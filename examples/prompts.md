# Opening prompts

The skill triggers on requests like these; the intake questions fill in whatever they leave open.

## Finding a topic (mathematics)

> I have a seminar on Thursday for my group in algebraic geometry and topology. Find a recent
> preprint suitable for 90 minutes, with non-trivial mathematics and something we can check in
> Lean. I'll use the board and a computer. Prepare notes, slides and a landing page, and keep a
> memory file, a journal, a plan, an Obsidian vault and a local git repository.

> Compare my interests with the arXiv preprints of the last two months in number theory and
> algebraic geometry and pick something properly mathematical, at most 15 pages.

## Steering the rounds

> I'm not convinced. Something about the technical issues of formalizing this area, and the new
> tactics?

> Maybe a paper from the last two months where tactic-heavy proofs were used, so that I can explain
> how such tactics are built.

> Yes, let's go with the second one. Formalize it as far as is reasonable, check its computations
> in Magma, write a Verso document, find any issues in the paper, and rewrite it for my notes and
> slides. All in one go.

## A paper you already have

> I'm presenting arXiv:XXXX.XXXXX at our reading group next week (60 minutes). Make a digest that
> says which claims you re-checked and how, re-run its computations in SageMath, and build slides
> with a board plan. No Lean this time.

## Computer science

> I give a talk on a new algorithms paper (arXiv:XXXX.XXXXX) at our theory seminar. Formalize the
> key invariant in Lean, re-run their experiments on our cluster, check the complexity claim
> empirically, and prepare slides and a short page for the participants.

> Our verification group reads an SMT paper next month. Reproduce two tables from the artifact,
> check the solvers' certificates, and give me a live demo that finds a counterexample to a
> weakened invariant.

## Delivery

> Keep the dashboard local for now; when the talk is over, publish it as a private Claude
> artifact.

> Publish the page to my server (dry run first).
